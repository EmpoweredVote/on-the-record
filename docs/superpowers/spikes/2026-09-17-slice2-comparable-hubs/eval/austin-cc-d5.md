slug: `austin-cc-d5`

**David Weinberg**, **Liz Brink**, **Farrah Abraham**. (Abraham's residency/district-eligibility was
publicly disputed in mid-2026 news coverage, but multiple sources confirm she is "officially on the
ballot for November.") No discrepancy with the task brief, unlike Race A.

## ⚠️ Search-engine trap encountered and corrected

Several searches and even a couple of AI-generated search summaries surfaced "Austin Chambers of
Commerce forum, District 5, Wednesday Sept. 21 at 5:30 p.m., Ann Richards School for Women Leaders,
moderated by KVUE's Bryan Mays" as if it were a 2026 event. Opening the source directly
(austinchamber.com press release and the KVUE article citing it) showed both are dated **2022**
(runoff-era Ann Kitchen open seat), not 2026. Similarly, KVUE's "Texas This Week" D5 interview and
KXAN's "Elevator Pitch"/"Meet the candidates" video pieces that surfaced in search are dated **2022**
(Bazan vs. Alter runoff). These are recorded below as unverified-for-2026 / stale, not as real 2026
sources — a good illustration of why every source needs to be opened, not just summarized from search.

## Richness summary

Better-resourced than the Bend race, as expected for a larger city, but still notably thinner than a
statewide or federal race. The clear standouts are the Community Impact written questionnaire (real,
filled, all 4 candidates, same 4 questions, verified) and the official City/Ethics Review
Commission/LWV candidate panel (real, government + neutral-nonprofit co-sponsored, airing live tonight
on ATXN.TV and KAZI FM, though not yet recorded/archived at research time). Ballotpedia's Candidate
Connection and the LWV/VOTE411 Voters Guide both have live infrastructure but are explicitly unfilled
for this race as of the research date. A full spoken debate/forum with all 4 candidates together, and
a comparable video interview series, could not be confirmed for 2026 — several promising-looking hits
(Chamber forum, KVUE, KXAN) turned out on inspection to be stale 2022 content resurfaced by search,
underscoring the value of opening every URL rather than trusting search summaries.
